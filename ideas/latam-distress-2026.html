BAKIDE & Raizen - Two restructurings, one principle: own the right seat in the capital structure and treat equity as optionality.

The Setup

Distressed investing in LatAm is not about picking the best company. It is about picking the best position within a company that is already in trouble. The market tends to price the stress at the entity level, but the real question is always more specific: where do you sit in the structure if things get messy?

These are two separate stories with different mechanics, but they share the same underlying logic. Both are cases where the bond is the more interesting seat and equity carries risks that are easy to underestimate from the outside.

What It Is

Braskem is one of the largest petrochemical companies in the Americas. The stress is not at the parent, it is at Braskem Idesa, the Mexico subsidiary, where two series of senior secured notes are now in active restructuring talks: the 2029s (7.45%) and the 2032s (6.99%).

In February 2026, Braskem Idesa missed the coupon on the 2032s. That confirmed what was already clear from the negotiation timeline: this is a workout, not a watch list. The company has put a formal proposal on the table.

Where to Sit

Both series are senior secured, which is the important part. The proposal treats them together, so owning a basket of both avoids making a false precision call on which one "wins."

The Company Proposal

Three paths on the table. Each one tells you something about the recovery range.

Option A
Exchange

New 7-year senior secured notes at 100% of principal, same collateral, 6.95% coupon. No interest accrues for the first five years.

Par recovery, long wait
Option B
Cash-out

55 cents on the dollar in cash. Clean exit, immediate liquidity. This number is the practical anchor for where distressed recovery sits.

55 cents recovery floor
Note
Cash cap

Maximum $400m available for cash-out elections. Not everyone can take cash, which matters for how the exchange vs. exit split shakes out.

$400m ceiling
Catalyst Map
01
Restructuring progress

A clear path to the exchange or cash-out mechanics. Any headline that reduces timeline uncertainty is positive for bond prices.

02
Governance reset

CADE approved the IG4–Novonor stake transfer with no conditions. Petrobras waived its buyout rights. Cleaner control structure improves capital discipline.

03
Brazil pricing tailwind

Brazil imposed five-year anti-dumping duties on PE imports from the US and Canada, a modest but real domestic pricing support for the parent.

04
Legal path clarity

Faster timeline, fewer surprises on the court or creditor side. The more procedural this becomes, the more the market can price it.

What Breaks It
  • The exchange turns coercive with weaker economics than the current proposal, the 55 cents anchor moves lower.
  • Mexico outcome bleeds into the Braskem parent more than the market currently expects.
  • Another year of weak petrochemical spreads delays cash recovery and extends the restructuring timeline.
  • Equity is a small optionality play, not a base case. Don't size it like one.
Preferred Expression
A
50/50 BAKIDE basket

Equal weight in 2029s and 2032s. Since the proposal treats both together, this avoids false precision. If the deal is creditor-friendly, both benefit. If it gets coercive, both are impacted the same way.

B
40/60 tilt toward 2032

Higher beta with more upside if the deal is constructive. The 2029s are marginally more defensive. Tilt based on how much upside you want versus how much protection you need.

C
Equity as a kicker only

Braskem equity can work if Mexico is contained and governance genuinely improves. Keep sizing minimal and treat it as upside optionality on the base bond trade, not the other way around.

What It Is

Raizen is Brazil's largest sugar and ethanol producer, a Shell and Cosan joint venture with about R$65bn in total debt now in an out-of-court restructuring process. Unlike Braskem Idesa, this is not a classic distressed workout — it is a sponsor-led recap: two large, motivated shareholders trying to prevent a full court process by injecting capital and restructuring on their terms.

Fitch cut Raizen to 'C' after the restructuring announcement. Creditors holding 47% of the unsecured debt have already signed on, with 90 days to build broader support. The market knows this is serious. What it doesn't know yet is how clean the resolution will be.

Scenario Ladder

This is a survival plus re-pricing trade. The bond is the cleaner seat in all three outcomes.

Bull
Consensual recap + sponsor injection

Shell and Cosan commit capital (R$3.5bn from Shell was discussed). Creditor support crosses the threshold. Bonds re-rate meaningfully from distressed levels. Equity survives but likely diluted — still a cleaner outcome than the alternatives.

Base
Slow process, headline-driven trading

The recap takes longer than expected. Bonds trade on creditor sign-on news and sponsor statement cadence. Equity stays volatile. The bond is still the better place to be — you get paid to wait.

Bear
Heavy debt-to-equity conversion

Sponsors push for a conversion that dilutes existing shareholders heavily. Equity takes the first hit and the biggest hit. Bonds are still impaired but remain the cleaner seat — you recover more than you would holding equity.

Preferred Instrument

The Raizen 6.25% Notes due 2032 (USD, issued 2025, with guarantees from the financing subsidiary). Long enough to benefit from spread compression if the recap succeeds, but not as exposed as very long-dated paper if the process drags. It is the middle seat — which in a recap situation is usually the right one.

RAIZ4 (equity) is a high-volatility option on a friendly outcome. It can survive the recap and still hurt current shareholders through dilution mechanics. If you own equity here, own it small and own it knowing you might be right on the company and still wrong on the trade.

Catalyst Map
01
Creditor sign-on beyond 47%

The 90-day window to build broader support is the next visible milestone. Any move above 50–60% meaningfully raises the probability of an out-of-court resolution.

02
Sponsor backstop confirmed

Shell and Cosan capital injection details and timing. The market needs to see actual commitments, not just discussions. Numbers and dates matter here.

03
Asset sale proceeds

Real cash from asset sales, not just announcements. Raizen has assets that can be monetized — seeing actual proceeds improves the liquidity picture and reduces the equity conversion risk.

04
Operating improvement

Evidence that cash generation is improving post-weather shock. This is more of a medium-term signal, but it matters for how the market prices the long-dated bonds.

What Breaks It
  • A harsher debt-for-equity swap than the market expects — equity holders absorb the worst of it, but bonds are not clean either.
  • Commodity downside or weak agro conditions extending the stress beyond what the recap covers.
  • Sponsor fatigue — if Shell or Cosan signal they are less committed than the market thinks, the entire framework re-prices.
  • Political noise slowing legal execution in a process that already needs speed to stay out-of-court.
The Bottom Line

Braskem Idesa is the cleaner distressed trade: own the secured bonds, keep equity as a small kicker. Raizen is the cleaner debt trade: the business may survive, but equity can survive in a way that still hurts shareholders. In both cases, the bond is the better seat.
